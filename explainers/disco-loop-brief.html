1 · What Disco actually is

One-liner: a two-sided commerce-media network. At (and now before) checkout on one brand's store, shoppers see personalized paid offers from other brands, matched by ML over a 160M-profile purchase graph. Every transaction is a media moment; Disco monetizes it.

The economics, concretely. Publishers get paid per load/interaction (~$0.50 per checkout offer, ~$0.25 per thank-you-page load — pure incremental margin on a page that earned nothing before). Advertisers pay only on outcomes (CPA/CPC/CPE) with 100% share of voice — no competing ad in the slot. Disco takes a cut of the flow; Justin: "every dollar we make is a rev-share with our partners." For calibration, Rokt — the category giant — publicly takes ~50% of ad spend. Middleman economics here are fat.

Identity is deterministic and first-party — hashed email (SHA-256) at authenticated checkout is the join key; "zero cookies, zero fingerprinting, zero PII." Cookie death and ATT are a tailwind, not a threat.

4 · The ML greenfield (the pitch)

The single most important fact (Justin, verbatim): today's entire ML is one advertiser→publisher relationship model — "does Levi's perform well on Gap? Nothing else. There's no other ML behind it." The 160M-profile purchase graph is completely unused. They know it. This role exists to fix it.

Constraints: ~200ms standard serving budget; Justin is open to a ~1,500ms lane for LLM-overlay decisions (he name-dropped Qwen). He wants someone who "bleeds the lines together" between millisecond ML decisioning and LLM tooling.

Opportunity map — walk in with these (value order)
  1. Shopper-level personalization — sequence models / embeddings over purchase histories → p(convert | shopper, offer, context), replacing the advertiser×publisher matrix. This is LUMOS-shaped: one foundation model over all behavioral data replacing point models. Purchase data is the richest behavioral signal there is — the 10-year thesis, verbatim.
  2. Explore/exploit for offer ranking — contextual bandits for cold-start; only ~1,000 offers, so exploration is cheap and payoff fast.
  3. Yield & auction optimization (DiscoMix) — bid shading, floor pricing, direct-sold vs network blending. Direct quant-trading mapping: budgeted allocation under uncertainty.
  4. CPA bidding + budget pacing control plane — the Dream11 "aggression parameter" story transplants 1:1: transparency + steerable autonomy for non-ML operators. Justin already loved this.
  5. Incrementality / LTV — uplift modeling ("did the offer cause the purchase?") = the measurement ammo GTM needs against Rokt.
  6. LLM overlays — per-shopper creative/CTA, semantic catalog matching, LLM priors for cold-start brands, agentic campaign setup ("launch in an hour").
  7. Identity resolution — entity matching across 160M deterministic profiles; graph quality is the moat.
  8. Rewarded app feed — consumer-scale real-time recommender.
  9. Closed-loop evals — self-tuning thresholds; you've built eval-gated pipelines.
  10. Offer quality / brand-safety scoring — protects the curated-premium positioning that separates them from spam.
Honest model-work check: real in-house training (ranking, embeddings, propensity, auction) on a genuinely rich proprietary dataset — but not LLM pre/post-training. It's decisioning/recsys ML + an LLM application layer. Known trade; comp + remote + greenfield ownership is why this is being pursued.

5 · Serving math you should be fluent in

The ranking objective

The core task is multi-objective ranking: not CTR, but expected yield under brand-safety guardrails:

Rank = p(click | u, c) × p(conv | u, c, click) × Bid

where u = user features from the identity graph, c = publisher context. Typical stack: two-tower retrieval for candidates, GBDTs or sequential models for final scoring, strict calibration, IPS replay for offline eval before A/B.

Feel it: make Offer A win the slot

Offer B is fixed: 2.5% click × 20% conv × $45 CPA bid = $0.225 expected value per impression. Raw CTR doesn't win — the product does.

The latency budget

Industry standard for post-purchase surfaces: end-to-end under 200ms so the receipt page never feels slow, with the ML pipeline itself usually budgeted ~130ms. Pre-computed embeddings live in a hot feature store (Redis-class); nothing heavy happens on the request path.
