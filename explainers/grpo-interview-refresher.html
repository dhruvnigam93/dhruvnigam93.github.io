2The fourteen questions

Interviewer's question in bold; your answer below the blue bar. Timing badge = how long the spoken answer should run.

Q1
“Walk me through the pipeline end to end. What did YOU build?”
⏱ 60–90 secrebuilt

We had a banter bot in production — users ask it to roast a player or a team — running on stock Llama 8B, and the responses were clean but not funny. Stock LLMs aren't trained to be engaging. So the bet was: if we can measure engagingness, we can optimize for it directly with RL.

Three stages, and I built all three. One: a reward model — DeBERTa-v3 with a scoring head, trained on preference pairs mined from Reddit sports subreddits, where upvotes are a proxy for engagingness. Two: GRPO post-training of Llama 8B with LoRA against that reward model, with a per-token KL penalty to the base model — on a single 80GB A100. Three: deployment — the tuned model served via vLLM, validated with a -per-arm A/B against the untuned model, which showed a lift in week-over-week feature retention.

I personally wrote the Reddit data pipeline, the reward model training, the GRPO training loop and config, and ran the A/B. The serving infra existed; I plugged the new model into it.

Shape: goal → 3 stages → ownership claim. Trap defused: “I vs we” — claim the three stages explicitly, concede the serving infra before being asked.
Q2
“State the reward model loss precisely.”
⏱ 20 sec⚠ broke in grill — took two attempts

One training example is a pair of comments from the same post. Upvote scores are normalized within the post to [0,1] — call them R1 and R2. The model does a forward pass on each (post + comment), outputs scores p1 and p2 through a sigmoid head. The loss is squared error on the margin: ((R1 − R2) − (p1 − p2))². So it's a margin regression, not Bradley-Terry — deliberately.

Rule: one training example, one formula, done. Do NOT ramble into two versions of the objective like last time.
Q3
“Why margin regression instead of Bradley-Terry?”
⏱ 30 secrebuilt — two reasons, this order

Bradley-Terry only preserves the ordering — it throws away how much better one response is. Reddit is one of the only preference sources where you get real-valued magnitudes, and comment quality follows a power law — the top comment isn't slightly better than the fifth, it's 50x better. I wanted the reward model to carry that resolution.

And it matters downstream: GRPO's advantage is (r − mean)/std within a group, which is an affine transform — so relative margins within the group survive. A reward model that gets magnitudes right gives better-shaped advantages across my 16 rollouts than a pure ranker would. The cost is that the targets are noisier than binary preferences — I accepted that trade.

Q4
“GRPO standardizes rewards — doesn't that destroy your absolute scale?”
⏱ 15 sec✓ keep as-is — you won this one

It destroys scale across prompts, not within a group. Standardization is affine — subtract mean, divide by std — so the relative magnitudes among my 16 rollouts are preserved exactly. That within-group resolution is precisely what the margin-trained reward model buys. Cross-prompt scale I genuinely don't need, because advantages never compare across prompts.

Q5
“Reddit upvotes measure timing and position, not quality. What did you do?”
⏱ 30 secrebuilt — own the limitation in sentence one

They're a noisy proxy — position bias and early-mover advantage are real, and I didn't fully remove them. What I did: only high-traffic sports subreddits, where heavy vote volume dilutes position effects; only posts older than six months, so scores had fully converged; and within-post normalization, so I never compare across threads. The residual bet was that with enough volume, comment quality dominates timing noise — and the downstream A/B is what ultimately validated that bet.

Shape: mitigation → residual risk → downstream validation. Do NOT list biases you “thought about” without saying what you did.

Q8
“Did you validate the reward model on policy-generated text?”
⏱ 40 sec⚠ worst answer of the grill — this exact shape, no deviation

No — and that's a real limitation: the RM was validated on held-out Reddit comments, which is a different distribution from policy rollouts. What I had instead were three compensating controls. One, I tracked reward on a held-out prompt set at every checkpoint during GRPO — if the RM were being gamed, held-out reward and output quality diverge, and I was reading the outputs at every checkpoint. Two, I monitored HellaSwag to catch capability regression. Three, the final arbiter was the A/B — real users, not the reward model. If I did it again, I'd label a few hundred policy generations mid-training and check RM agreement in exactly the regime GRPO uses it — discriminating between same-policy samples, where margins are small.

Follow-up you must expect — “Is 92% suspiciously high?”: Fair — random pairing across the full upvote range means many easy pairs; the top-comment-vs-throwaway pair inflates the average. Stratified by margin, accuracy on close pairs would be meaningfully lower. If you have the stratified number, give it: on closest-quartile pairs. If not, say you'd measure exactly that.
Q9
“Why GRPO over DPO? You already had preference pairs.”
⏱ 45 sec⚠ THE rebuild — distribution shift FIRST, margins second

Because DPO would have optimized the wrong distribution. DPO trains directly on my preference pairs — and my pairs are Reddit comments on Reddit posts. Running DPO on that data pushes Llama toward imitating upvoted Reddit comments — off-policy imitation of text my bot should never produce verbatim. What I actually want is to improve the policy's own generations. The reward model is the generalization step: it learns what engaging looks like from Reddit, and then GRPO applies that judgment to on-policy rollouts.

Second-order reason: DPO's implicit reward only uses orderings; my margin-trained RM preserves magnitude, which sharpens within-group advantages.

And GRPO specifically over PPO because it drops the value network — no critic to train or fit in memory, which mattered on a single A100.

In the grill: you gave the margins argument three times and never said “distribution shift.” The interviewer had to hand it to you. This answer gets double reps.

Q11
“State the GRPO update. Advantage, per-token loss, clipping, KL, your numbers.”
⏱ 45 sec⚠ depth check — said G=16 then G=12 in one answer, no β

One prompt, G = 16 rollouts sampled from the current policy. Each rollout gets a scalar reward from the RM. Advantage for rollout i is (rᵢ − group mean) / group std — same advantage applied to every token of that rollout. Per-token loss is the clipped policy-gradient objective — importance ratio times advantage — but in my setup I took exactly one optimizer step per rollout batch, so the policy that generated equals the policy being updated, the ratio is identically 1, and clipping never binds. It's pure on-policy REINFORCE with group-normalized advantages.

KL enters the objective directly — a per-token KL penalty to the reference policy, weighted by β = . Not folded into the reward like classic PPO-RLHF — it's a separate additive term in the loss.

Config: LoRA rank , α on the linear layers — the adapter output BAx is scaled by α/r before being added back to the frozen path, so α/r sets the effective adapter strength — LR , prompts per batch, trained for steps on one 80GB A100.

Deliver it like a recipe you've cooked 50 times. The config is the ownership proof — “I built this end to end” + config amnesia reads as inflation even when it isn't.
Q12
“What happened when the KL weight was wrong?”
⏱ 30 sec✓ your best answer — keep the war story

We launched without a KL term at all, and got entropy collapse — the policy found a degenerate mode, repeating near-identical high-reward phrasing, and output entropy cratered into gibberish. That's what taught us the KL term isn't optional. We added the per-token penalty and tuned β up until generation stayed diverse while reward still climbed. And because reward-vs-quality can silently diverge, I also monitored HellaSwag across checkpoints as a canary — I picked it over MMLU deliberately: a banter bot doesn't need grad-school knowledge, it needs common-sense grounding, and HellaSwag is exactly the common-sense completion regime I was worried about degrading. It held flat at .

Watch: in the grill you said “MMLU” under pressure — the more famous benchmark name. The HellaSwag version is the true one AND the better answer. Close the scripted-vs-lived gap on this line specifically.

F6
“Standard RLHF samples preference pairs from the model itself. Yours came from Reddit. Why not collect preferences on your own model's outputs?”

Own it. Canonical preference data — InstructGPT, Llama 2 — is sampled from the SFT policy precisely so the RM is on-distribution for what it scores. Mine is off-policy preference data, same family as the SHP dataset (Stanford Human Preferences — also built from Reddit scores). The reason: no labeling budget or annotation team for K-way human rankings of model samples; Reddit gave me 222K pairs for free with a real-user engagement signal, which is closer to my actual objective — engagingness — than contractor ratings would be. The cost is exactly the Q8 limitation: the RM is off-distribution for policy outputs. With budget, the upgrade is: sample pairs from the policy mid-training, label them, refresh the RM — iterated RLHF.

★
The Hinglish emergence story
⏱ 30 sec✓ pocket story — always loaded

The most interesting emergent behavior: on Indian-cricket prompts, the policy started responding in Hinglish even when the prompt was pure English. Base Llama never does that — instruct models mirror the prompt's language. But in Indian cricket subreddits, the highest-engagement comments are Hinglish — code-switching IS the banter register — so the reward model scored Hinglish rollouts higher, and GRPO amplified it until the policy broke language-mirroring. That's the clearest evidence the pipeline optimized engagingness rather than imitating a style: nothing in the SFT prior produces this. The reward signal taught it. And it survived the A/B — that register is part of what drove the retention lift.
