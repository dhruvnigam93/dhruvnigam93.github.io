Running example throughout: a 15-step debugging agent (read code → run tests → edit → re-run tests). One terminal reward: final test suite passes. Good diagnosis in steps 1–6, fatal edit at step 9.

1. The Two Structural Problems

GRPO single-turn is a token-level loss with episode-level attribution — one scalar broadcast to every token.

Problem A: Sparsity

Long horizon → early policy never succeeds → all G rollouts in a group fail → advantages are all zero → no gradient at all. Not weak signal; literally zero. Training stalls before it begins.

Problem B: Credit Attribution

Even when signal exists, one terminal scalar is shared uniformly across 15 steps. A trajectory with 14 perfect steps and 1 fatal edit is indistinguishable from one that was wrong everywhere. But it gets worse than "indistinguishable" — the uniform broadcast is actively harmful. That's section 2.

Why these compound at long horizons

Short horizons (3–5 steps) are forgiving: the policy stumbles into successes often enough that groups have variance, and broadcasting one scalar over 5 tokens causes only mild misattribution. At 15 steps, success probability drops exponentially with horizon length, and the broadcast error grows linearly. Both problems bite simultaneously.

2. Uniform Broadcast = Wrong-Sign Signal

Your initial answer ("just no signal") was wrong. It is not neutral-but-sparse. A failed trajectory's uniform negative advantage does active gradient descent on the flawless diagnosis steps 1–6. Good sub-behavior is punished because it co-occurred with step 9's mistake.

Consider: the debugging agent reads code carefully (steps 1–6, all correct), then makes a fatal edit at step 9. The episode fails. Under GRPO, the entire trajectory gets a uniform negative advantage. Every token — including those encoding the perfect diagnosis — gets pushed down. The policy learns: "don't do that diagnosis." But the diagnosis was right.

3. How a Critic Localizes Blame

Your weakest conceptual item. You said "the critic is an intelligent model that figures it out" — a black-box appeal that fails in interviews. The mechanism is one equation.

δt = rt + γV(st+1) − V(st)

TD error: how much better was the next state than expected?

Concrete numbers for the debugging agent

V climbs 0.2 → 0.7 across steps 1–6 (each δt > 0 → positive advantage on good steps inside a FAILED trajectory). V craters 0.7 → 0.05 at step 9 (δ9 ≈ −0.65 — the most negative TD error; blame concentrates exactly where value drops). δ ≈ 0 for steps 10–15 (mistake already priced in — neither blamed nor credited).

GAE: dialing between critic and outcome

GAE(λ) blends TD errors at different horizons. λ dials between trusting local δ's (λ→0, trusts the critic) and outcome broadcast (λ→1, trusts the environment).

Anchor phrase: "Bootstrap = bias, sample = variance; trust the one that isn't broken."

Garbage critic? Raise λ toward 1 (trust the environment). Good critic? Lower λ toward 0 (trust the bootstrap, get lower variance).

4. Four Credit-Assignment Schemes

Scheme 1: Outcome-only + GRPO (baseline)

Signal: terminal reward (pass/fail), group-normalized across G rollouts, same advantage broadcast to every agent token.

Fixes: nothing about attribution — this IS the baseline. Ungameable. Zero design cost. Verifiable.

Failure: both structural problems at full strength. All-fail groups → zero advantage → no gradient. When signal exists, it is uniform: good steps in failed trajectories get punished.

Pick it when: short horizons, or the policy already succeeds often enough that groups have variance. First thing to try; insufficient at 15 steps early in training.

Scheme 2: Step-delta process reward

Signal: rewardt = Δ(tests passing) after each edit. Free from the environment; no judge, no critic.

Fixes: density. Every edit gets local, verifiable feedback.

TWO distinct failure modes (you merged them today — keep them separate):

(a) Myopic shaping / valley punishment: Removing a hacky patch that masks the root cause drops pass count 7/14 → 4/14. The single most important edit gets negative reward. The agent never learns to explore paths that get worse before better.

(b) Metric gaming: Patch-per-test-case stacking instead of root-cause fix. The agent learns to game the proxy metric rather than solve the problem.

Nuance for your pocket: deltas telescope (sum = final − initial passes), so the return is path-independent — it's the per-step advantage that punishes the valley.

Scheme 3: Hindsight necessity / Shapley over edits

Signal: post-hoc, on successful trajectories only. Ablate subsets of edits, re-run the test suite. Characteristic function = test-suite run.

Full Shapley: 2k runs. Leave-one-out: linear, but its blind spot is redundant edits (either alone suffices → removing one changes nothing → BOTH marked unnecessary). Jointly-necessary pairs are handled fine — don't confuse these.

Edits may not commute (edit 4 touches lines edit 2 created) — arbitrary subsets aren't always valid patches.

Critical limitation: post-hoc on successes only. Refines credit within successful trajectories. Does nothing for cold start.

Scheme 4: ARPO-style branched rollouts

Signal: branch each rollout at selected steps into multiple continuations → execution tree. Leaves carry terminal rewards. Each shared prefix gets the mean of its descendants' advantages; branches keep their own.

YOUR RECURRING ARITHMETIC BUG: branches {+1, −1, 0} → prefix gets 0 (the mean), NOT 1/3 (you said 1/3 today). And ≈0 is the correct, meaningful answer: the prefix didn't decide the outcome.

Deep framing: the descendant-mean is a Monte Carlo estimate of V(s) at the branch point — ARPO rebuilds the critic empirically, paying rollout compute instead of training a value model.

Where to branch: at high-entropy points (entropy spikes right after tool feedback — that's where the agent is uncertain about what to do next).

5. Advantage Normalization: Where Do the Statistics Come From?

YOUR WORST ITEM — failed two days running. Pay maximum attention here. The three choices differ in what population the mean and std are estimated from, and the defects are not interchangeable.

Choice A: Per-question group (standard GRPO, G=16)

Advantagei = (Ri − mean) / std, computed from 16 rollouts of the same prompt.

Defect = NOISY DENOMINATOR. std estimated from only 16 samples. Near-uniform group → std → 0 → tiny noise divides by almost-nothing → colossal advantages.

Concrete: easy task, 15/16 correct. The lone failure's advantage = (0 − 0.9375) / (tiny std) = enormous negative. That is amplified noise, not meaningful signal.

Direction you keep getting backwards: the blow-up is at near-zero variance, not high variance.

Second effect: fully-degenerate groups (all 16 same result) contribute zero gradient → participation-rate imbalance across tasks. Hard tasks (where the policy rarely succeeds) drop out of the gradient diet. That is absence, not domination.

Choice B: Global pool

Defect = MIXED CURRENCIES. One shared std preserves relative family scales. The wide-scale family (0–10 judge scores) dominates; binary code rewards (0 or 1) get crushed.

THIS is where "high-variance tasks dominate the gradient" is a correct statement. You keep aiming it at per-question, where it's impossible — dividing each group by its own std erases scale per group.

Choice C: Per-task/environment pool (ERPO / AgentRL)

Pool all rollouts of the same task type (e.g., all code tasks, all QA tasks). Stable denominator (hundreds of samples), single currency within a family.

Cost: low-spread tails within a family (very easy / very hard questions) under-train. Related: Dr. GRPO drops /std entirely.

Print this verbatim: "The mean answers 'better than my siblings?'; the std is the exchange rate — estimate it from a population you can trust (task pool), not 16 samples (per-question), and never across mixed currencies (global)."

6. Cross-Policy Sampling (AgentRL)

Disease: all G rollouts converge to near-identical trajectories → within-group variance ≈ 0 → learning stalls.

Fix: during rollout generation, inject turns from older checkpoints into the trajectory.

Why it beats raising temperature:

Temperature adds noise at the token level. Random tokens rarely compose into a coherent alternative strategy — they break syntax/reasoning. A checkpoint is a complete coherent policy, so its turns are samples of valid strategies — diversity at the strategy level.

ε-greedy analogue with plausible behavior instead of random actions.

Pairing to remember:

  • Task-level normalization fixes cross-task gradient imbalance
  • Cross-policy sampling fixes within-task exploration collapse

Same paper (AgentRL), two diseases.
