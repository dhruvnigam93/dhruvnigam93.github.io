GRPO, step by step

The training loop, the counting (batch → rollouts → updates → optimizer steps), and where the KL actually lives — then the real production run (Banter Bot at Dream11) and the reward model behind it. Built to kill the confusions that resurface every time.

1 · The cast

Three models, one reward function — keep their jobs separate

Almost every GRPO confusion is really a confusion about which model a quantity is computed against. Fix these four roles first.

πθ — the policy
TRAINABLE · changes every optimizer step

The model you're improving. The only thing gradients ever touch.

Lifespan of its weights: one optimizer step.
πold — the sampler
FROZEN SNAPSHOT · refreshed every generation cycle

A copy of πθ taken at rollout time. It generated the completions, so the importance ratio and the clip are measured against it.

Lifespan: one generation cycle (μ update iterations).
πref — the anchor
FROZEN FOREVER · usually the SFT checkpoint

Never updated. The KL penalty is measured against it — "don't forget who you were before RL."

Lifespan: the whole run. (Dropped entirely when β = 0.)
R — the reward
REWARD MODEL or PROGRAMMATIC CHECK

Scores each finished completion with one scalar. In verifiable domains (math, code) it's just a correctness check.

One number per completion. No critic. No value model. Anywhere.

4 · The KL question

Two comparisons, two different jobs — and only one of them is a KL

At every single token, the current policy is compared against two other models. Conflating these two comparisons is the classic GRPO confusion.

one token position t in completion oi
P(“204” | prefix)
πold0.40
πθ (now)0.46
πref0.31
ratio rt = 0.46 / 0.40 = 1.15
vs πold → fed into the clipped surrogate. Trust region: “don't move too far per rollout batch.” Not a KL term.
KLt ≈ 0.052 (k3 estimator)
vs πref → multiplied by β, added to the loss. Regularizer: “don't drift from the SFT model.” This is the only KL.

Where does the KL go? PPO-RLHF vs GRPO

PPO-RLHF (InstructGPT): KL(πθ‖πref) is subtracted from the reward per token — rt = rRM − β·KL — and then advantages come out of GAE with a learned critic. The KL contaminates the advantage estimate.

GRPO: the KL is pulled out of the reward and added as a separate per-token term directly in the loss. The advantage stays a pure, group-normalized reward signal.

The estimator (what the code computes per token, no logits over the full vocab needed):
kl = exp(ref_logp − θ_logp) − (ref_logp − θ_logp) − 1
Schulman's k3 — unbiased, always ≥ 0.

Nobody computes a KL to πold. The πold comparison is the ratio + clip. If you say “KL with the old policy” in an interview, you've merged two different mechanisms.

7 · The real run

This exact loop, in production — Banter Bot at Dream11

Everything above ran for real: GRPO post-training of Llama 8B (LoRA) so a fantasy-sports banter bot would stop being clean-but-boring. Stock LLMs aren't trained to be engaging; the bet was that if engagingness can be measured, it can be optimized directly with RL. Three stages, one 80 GB A100.

Stage 1 — reward model
MEASURE ENGAGINGNESS

DeBERTa-v3 with a scoring head, trained on 222K preference pairs mined from high-traffic Reddit sports subreddits — upvotes as the proxy for engagingness. Full anatomy in section 8 below.

Output: one scalar per completion — the R in the loop above.
Stage 2 — GRPO post-training
OPTIMIZE FOR IT

Llama 8B + LoRA on the linear layers, G = 16 rollouts per prompt, per-token KL to the reference, one optimizer step per rollout batch. Rollout prompts: the Reddit posts themselves, LLM-judge-filtered to look like production queries ("roast X", "what do you think of Y").

μ = 1, one step per batch ⇒ ratio ≡ 1 ⇒ clip never binds.
Stage 3 — deploy & validate
REAL USERS ARE THE ARBITER

Tuned model served via vLLM. A/B against the untuned model, ~150K users per arm, on week-over-week feature retention (used the bot in week 1 → used it again in week 2).

Lift: +16% relative on the resume; grill recall was 19.5%→22% (≈ +12.8%) — reconcile against the dashboard before quoting either.
Symbol aboveWhat it was in this run

⚠War story 1 — entropy collapse

First launch had no KL term at all. The policy found a degenerate mode: near-identical high-reward phrasing repeated everywhere, output entropy cratering into gibberish. That's what the KL is for — the per-token anchor to πref went in, β tuned up until diversity held while reward still climbed. "The KL isn't optional" is a lesson this run paid for.

☂War story 2 — the HellaSwag canary

Reward-vs-quality can diverge silently, so a capability benchmark ran at every checkpoint. HellaSwag over MMLU, deliberately: a banter bot doesn't need grad-school knowledge, it needs common-sense grounding — HellaSwag is exactly the common-sense completion regime most at risk. It held flat throughout.

★War story 3 — emergent Hinglish

On Indian-cricket prompts the policy started replying in Hinglish even when the prompt was pure English. Instruct models mirror the prompt's language — nothing in the SFT prior produces this. But on Indian cricket subreddits the highest-engagement comments are Hinglish; the RM scored it higher and GRPO amplified it until language-mirroring broke. The clearest evidence the pipeline optimized engagingness rather than imitating a style — and that register survived into the A/B win.

8 · The reward model

DeBERTa-v3 + margin regression — where R actually comes from

GRPO consumes one scalar per rollout and never asks where it came from. In this run it came from a deliberately non-standard choice: a margin-regression reward model instead of Bradley-Terry — a bet that only makes sense once you remember what the group-normalized advantage preserves.

Architecture
ENCODER + SCORING HEAD

DeBERTa-v3 — encoder with disentangled content/position attention, v3 pretrained ELECTRA-style with replaced-token detection. Input: post + comment in one forward pass. Scoring head through a sigmoid → p ∈ [0,1].

One forward pass per comment; the pair loss compares two passes.
Data
REDDIT AS A PREFERENCE ORACLE

~1,200 posts from high-traffic sports subreddits, only posts > 6 months old (scores fully converged), ~20 comments sampled per post (capped so no thread dominates). All within-post pairs: C(20,2) = 190 per post × ~1,200 posts ≈ 228K → 222K after dedup. Upvotes normalized within-post to [0,1] — never compared across threads.

Off-policy preference data — same family as Stanford Human Preferences (SHP).
Validation
SPLIT AT THE POST LEVEL

Pair-level splitting would leak — every comment appears in ~19 pairs. So the split is by post, with held-out posts for validation. Metric: pairwise ranking accuracy on held-out pairs ≈ 92% — with a caveat: random pairing across the full upvote range makes many pairs easy. Stratified by margin, accuracy on close pairs is meaningfully lower.

Known limitation: never validated on policy-generated text (see below).

The loss — margin regression, not Bradley-Terry

One training example = two comments from the same post, targets R₁, R₂ ∈ [0,1] (normalized upvotes), model scores p₁, p₂. Loss:

ℓ = ((R₁ − R₂) − (p₁ − p₂))²

Bradley-Terry (−log σ(s₁ − s₂)) preserves only the ordering — it throws away how much better one response is. Reddit is one of the only preference sources with real-valued magnitudes, and comment quality is power-law distributed: the top comment isn't slightly better than the fifth, it's 50× better. Margin regression keeps that resolution.

Why it matters downstream: GRPO's advantage (r − mean)/std is an affine transform within the group — relative margins among the 16 rollouts survive standardization exactly. A magnitude-aware RM gives better-shaped advantages than a pure ranker. The cost, accepted: targets are noisier than binary preferences.

The honest no — and the three compensating controls

The RM was validated on held-out Reddit comments — a different distribution from policy rollouts. That's a real limitation, owned out loud. What stood in for it: (1) reward on a held-out prompt set tracked at every GRPO checkpoint, with outputs read by hand — if the RM were being gamed, held-out reward and visible quality diverge; (2) the HellaSwag canary for capability regression; (3) the A/B as final arbiter — real users, not the reward model. The upgrade if done again: label a few hundred policy generations mid-training and check RM agreement exactly where GRPO uses it — discriminating between same-policy samples, where margins are small.

9 · Say these out loud

Eight lines that signal depth in an interview

1Advantage is per-completion, not per-token

One scalar per rollout — (R − group mean)/group std — broadcast to every token in that completion. GRPO has no per-token credit assignment from a critic; the only per-token quantities are the ratio and the KL.

2Two comparisons, two jobs

Ratio + clip vs πold = trust region (safe rollout reuse). KL vs πref = regularizer (don't drift from SFT). There is no KL to the old policy, ever.

3At μ = 1 the clip is dead machinery

First update after generation: πθ = πold, every ratio is exactly 1, min/clip do nothing — GRPO reduces to vanilla policy gradient with a group baseline. The PPO machinery only wakes up when rollouts are reused (μ > 1, or the batch consumed over multiple optimizer steps).

4All-same rewards ⇒ the group is dead weight

If all G rollouts get the same reward (all correct or all wrong), std ≈ 0 and every advantage is 0 — zero gradient from that prompt. This is why DAPO does dynamic sampling (resample until groups are mixed) and why prompt difficulty curation matters so much.

5No critic is the whole point

PPO needs a separate value model (≈ policy-sized) trained alongside. GRPO's baseline is just the group mean — that's roughly half the memory and none of the value-loss instability. The price: you need G samples per prompt to estimate it.

6Token averaging is a live design choice

GRPO averages per-sequence then across the group — which under-weights tokens in long completions (length bias). Dr.GRPO and DAPO average over all tokens in the batch instead. In TRL it's literally a flag: loss_type. Also: β = 0 is common now — drop πref, save a forward pass and the memory.

7Margin regression because standardization is affine

Group normalization subtracts the mean and divides by the std — relative magnitudes within the group survive exactly. A Bradley-Terry ranker throws magnitude away at training time; a margin-trained RM carries it through to the advantages. Cross-prompt scale is destroyed, and that's fine — advantages never compare across prompts.

8The KL earned its place — we launched without it

No KL term → entropy collapse: a degenerate high-reward mode, then gibberish. The per-token anchor to πref is what keeps reward-climbing from becoming mode-collapse. And monitor a capability canary (HellaSwag here) — reward and quality can diverge without either curve telling you.
